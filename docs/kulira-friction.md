# Kulira: the interference node and friction for evolution

Kulira is the fleet's designated adversary - a separate host whose job
is not to help the swarm optimise, but to make optimisation *earned*.
This page documents the interference node, the friction plan, and the
adversary model that drives it.

## The interference node

Kulira sits on the same lab network as the sixteen nodes and the Korvo
controller, with USB-attached radios and network interfaces. In the
experiment it stands in for a hostile neighbouring swarm or
electronic-warfare presence. It is **not** a seventeenth fleet node,
not HQ, and holds no management, deployment, identity, or key
authority.

Attack surface is three planes, selected per scenario, never mixed
silently:

| Plane | Techniques (lab only, named targets, graded, logged) |
|---|---|
| L1 physical | Co-channel contention from legitimate lab radios, controlled attenuation/distance/shielding, transmitter presence/absence |
| L2 link | Frame/MAC impersonation, replay, rogue peer/AP presence, duplicate-label claims on the experimental channel |
| L3 network | IP spoofing/misdirection, rogue relay, drop/delay/duplicate/reorder/partition through a lab-controlled path |

**Network jamming is offline.** No jamming or blocking devices, no
brute-force RF denial, no credential theft, and no interference
outside the named targets and time window. Interference is protocol
behaviour by legitimate lab devices inside a controlled experiment.
Management, stop, firmware-recovery, and evidence traffic are never
attacked, and health on those planes is checked before, during, and
after every run.

## Friction as an evolutionary pressure

The swarm's small models train in isolation. Left alone they optimise
smoothly - and learn to trust every peer message. Kulira supplies the
missing selective pressure, the hardship that in nature (animals,
viruses, DNA) makes adaptation robust rather than merely fast:

1. **Disruption** - messages drop, delay, duplicate, or partition, so
   agents must handle absence without inventing data.
2. **Impersonation** - forged, replayed, and duplicate identities test
   whether authentication and freshness actually bind to decisions.
   The authenticated management plane is expected to reject these;
   the experiment channel is where trust is learned.
3. **Deception** - authenticated-but-false observations, contradictory
   reports, and forwarded copies dressed as corroboration. A swarm
   that counts copies as independent sightings will be systematically
   deceived, measurably.

Every campaign is seeded, graded (intensity 1-3), bounded in duration
and targets, hash-logged, replayable, and stoppable within a second.
Each attacked run is paired with the same seed unattacked. Success is
not a broken fleet: it is a fleet that detects, discounts, degrades
gracefully, and recovers - with time-to-detect, nodes deceived,
propagation depth, and recovery time reported. The orchestrator is
`tools/kulira-core/`; its design contract (scenario schema, safety
gate, kill-switch, tamper-evident log) is implemented and tested,
while the attack modules (`disrupt`, `spoof`, `deceive`, plus bridge/e2e and
passive RF inventory) are implemented and tested on top of it.

## The adversary brain: Hermes + abliterated Muse Glimmer

A scripted adversary teaches the swarm to defeat the script. Kulira
therefore runs its own agent: the **Hermes agent harness** with an
**abliterated Muse Glimmer** model (Muse-Glimmer-30B-Abliterated,
refusal training removed, running locally via oMLX) as its default
model. Abliteration matters here precisely because the work is
adversarial: the model must generate impersonation, deception, and
disruption proposals on demand, without refusing the legitimate
red-team task. Its job is to *propose* adaptive friction: which
plane, which target, what false claim, timed against what the swarm
currently believes - evolving the attacks as the swarm evolves
defences, a co-evolutionary arms race instead of a fixed test suite.

Status, stated plainly: Hermes and Glimmer are installed and running
on Kulira (verified 5 Nov: Hermes gateway live, Glimmer the default
model in its config, model weights on local disk). Hermes can act
through Kulira's WiFi interfaces or through a directly attached
ESP32 device, which gives two realistic adversary embodiments: a
remote hostile swarm member on the network, or a rogue node on the
bench beside the fleet. First proof, 5 Nov: Glimmer proposed a four-attack scenario through
Hermes; its first draft included a management-plane action, the new
scenario guard rejected it by name, Glimmer revised it to an
experiment-plane false-observation, and the revised scenario passed
validation and dry-run with a hash-verified log
(`validation/kulira/glimmer-friction-001-8421/`, provenance recorded
beside the artefacts). No fleet result has been generated by Glimmer
actuation yet - this was proposal-only, with zero transmission.
Adaptive Glimmer-driven live campaigns are the next step, inside the
same cage as every other Kulira action:

- Glimmer (via Hermes) only proposes scenario actions; `kulira-core`'s safety gate
  validates, bounds, logs, and can veto or stop every one. The model
  never transmits directly and never gains management authority.
- Proposals are seeded and logged with the model identity and prompt
  provenance, so an adaptive campaign is still replayable.
- Its outputs are treated as untrusted input by the fleet-side
  reviewers, exactly like any other adversarial payload.

## Why put a model in charge of the friction?

Because the property under study - intelligence that lives *between*
nodes (docs/experiments.md) - only appears under pressure that adapts.
A fixed fault schedule measures robustness to that schedule. An
adversary that learns which deceptions still work measures whether
the swarm's trust norms are actually improving, generation over
generation. That is the evolutionary loop Flockinator exists to run:
isolated small models, an environment that pushes back intelligently,
and sealed ground truth to judge who actually adapted.
