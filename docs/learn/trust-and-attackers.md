# Trust, scepticism, and attackers

![Same message, three meanings](trust-attacks.svg)

If shared intelligence lives in messages, then the most important
question a node can ask is not "what did I hear?" but:

> "Should I believe it - yet?"

Healthy swarms are not simply trusting. Completely trusting groups
believe the first liar. Completely doubting groups never share
anything and lose the whole benefit. The sweet spot is
**sceptical communication**:

- Believe a signed message came from who it claims - then ask
  whether what it says is true. Those are different questions.
- Count **independent** sightings, not copies. Ten repeats of one
  report are still one report.
- Let urgency matter. A fast decision may need to act on less
  proof than a slow one - and should know it took the risk.
- Remember who was wrong before, without freezing anyone out
  forever.

Attackers know all this. They will impersonate a trusted node,
replay an old true message at a dangerous new moment, copy one
false sighting until it looks like everyone agrees, delay the
correction until after the decision, or simply stay silent when
their honest report would have helped. Flockinator's adversary,
[Kulira](../kulira-friction.md) — the controlled adversary node that lies, impersonates, and disrupts on purpose — exists to do exactly these things
in a safe lab, on purpose, with a scorekeeper watching.

We cannot defend only against attacks we can list. The deeper
defence is a swarm whose habits - doubt, corroboration, saying
"I don't know yet" - work even against surprises. That is what
the experiments are training, and testing, generation after
generation. See the [attack taxonomy](../attack-taxonomy.md) for
the full catalogue and the surprises we have already met.
